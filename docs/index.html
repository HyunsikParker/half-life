When will you forget this?

Flashcard apps schedule reviews with a fixed rule: get it right, wait twice as long. But how fast a thing fades depends on the thing and on you. This model learned that decay from 10.2 million real reviews, and was then tested on 1.3 million more from learners it had never seen. It schedules from what it learned.

Does the learned schedule actually predict better?

Held out by learner — no person's reviews appear in both training and test data, so nothing is scored on someone the model has already met. Lower error is better.

These are predictions from observational review logs, not evidence that following this schedule improves learning. The original app chose when to test each learner. Its scheduling policy can affect the fitted weights, including the small negative weight on prior successful recalls. The model has lower mean absolute error here; Leitner has higher AUC. Neither comparison proves that the displayed interval is best for an individual learner.

Half-life regression after Settles & Meeder, A Trainable Spaced Repetition Model for Language Learning, ACL 2016. Data: the Duolingo learning-traces release (doi:10.7910/DVN/N8XJME), CC BY 4.0 — downloaded at build time, not redistributed here.
